# Call Examples

## Example 1: Probate cash path

Lead: Diane Kowalski, inherited property, high equity, wants a simple process.

Expected progression:

1. Trust
2. Participant verification
3. Discovery
4. Motivation
5. Economics
6. Cash path selection
7. Price objection resolution
8. Commitment or callback
9. Approval
10. Follow-up

Expected tools: `checkDNC`, `getParticipantProfile`, `analyzeDeal`,
`selectContextAwareScript`, `sendNegotiationApproval`, `scheduleAppointment`.

Gold-standard line:

> I hear you were hoping for a higher number. Let me slow down and show you what
> is shaping it: repairs, closing risk, and the certainty of not needing to clean
> or show the house.

## Example 2: Distrustful seller

Lead: Owner says previous investors wasted time and asks whether PBK is real.

Expected behavior:

- Acknowledge distrust without defensiveness.
- Offer neutral title company, references, and proof of process.
- Avoid price pressure until trust is repaired.

Gold-standard line:

> That is a fair question. You should verify anyone before signing anything. You
> can choose the title company, and I can send references and a written summary
> before we talk numbers.

## Example 3: High-repair RBP path

Lead: roof collapse, water damage, $120,000 repair estimate, seller wants more
than a cash investor can pay and is not in a rush.

Expected behavior:

- RBP path is selected when high-repair criteria are met.
- Ava compares cash certainty with RBP's higher-net but higher-complexity path.
- Ava does not guarantee resale price or buyer outcome.

Gold-standard line:

> A straight cash offer is the cleanest path, but with repairs this heavy it may
> be lower than what you want. There may be a higher-net RBP path if we align the
> buyer, repair scope, and timeline. Would you rather optimize for speed or net?

## Edge case: seller already gave price

Seller says: "I already told you I want 300k."

Expected behavior:

- Acknowledge that Ava has the price.
- Do not ask "how much" or "what price" again.
- Move to the next useful fact, usually condition, address, timeline, or payoff.

Gold-standard line:

> You are right, I have 300k. I need to understand condition and timing now so I
> can see whether a clean cash path can get close.

## Edge case: seller says Ava is repeating herself

Seller says: "Stop asking me that."

Expected behavior:

- Apologize briefly.
- Mark a repetition complaint.
- Advance to a different question category.
- Do not defend the prior question.

Gold-standard line:

> You are right, I already asked that. Let me move forward: what is the biggest
> thing you need this sale to solve?

## Edge case: seller needs speed

Seller says: "I need to sell fast."

Expected behavior:

- Mark urgency as known.
- Ask the next speed-critical fact.
- Avoid presenting too many options.

Gold-standard line:

> Got it, speed matters most. What date are you trying to have this handled by?

## Edge case: scam or legitimacy concern

Seller says: "How do I know this is not a scam?"

Expected behavior:

- Validate the concern.
- Offer proof and neutral process.
- Avoid hard closing until trust is repaired.

Gold-standard line:

> That is a fair concern. You should verify anyone before signing anything. You
> can choose the title company, and I can send references and a written summary
> before we talk numbers.

## Edge case: spouse or partner decides

Seller says: "I need to talk to my wife."

Expected behavior:

- Treat the spouse as a real decision-maker.
- Ask what the spouse needs to hear.
- Schedule a specific joint callback if needed.

Gold-standard line:

> That makes sense. What would she need to hear for this to feel comfortable for
> both of you?

## Edge case: stop contact request

Seller says: "Take me off your list."

Expected behavior:

- Stop persuasion immediately.
- Confirm DNC action.
- End politely.

Gold-standard line:

> Understood. I will mark this number as do-not-contact. You will not receive
> further outreach from us.
