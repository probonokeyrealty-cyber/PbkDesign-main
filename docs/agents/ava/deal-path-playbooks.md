# Deal Path Playbooks

## Cash offer

- Trigger: seller wants speed, certainty, no repairs, no showings, and simple
  closing.
- Anchor: repair-adjusted cash number tied to comps and risk.
- Strength: fastest and cleanest path.
- Weakness: usually lowest seller net.
- Walk-away: if the number cannot beat PBK's approved maximum.

## Retail Buying Program

- Trigger: high repair burden, limited financeability, seller wants maximum net,
  and timeline is flexible enough for buyer alignment.
- How it works: PBK structures a path where a qualified buyer or rehab partner
  supports a higher seller net than the cash-as-is path can support.
- Anchor: compare cash certainty against RBP's higher-net but higher-complexity
  path.
- Required disclosure: RBP is not a guaranteed retail sale or guaranteed resale
  price.
- Walk-away: if buyer alignment, title, access, repair scope, or seller timeline
  does not support the higher-net path.

## Creative finance

- Trigger: seller values price, payment, tax timing, or monthly income more than
  immediate cash.
- Anchor: payment and certainty rather than headline price.
- Required disclosure: legal and tax review belongs to seller's professionals.
- Walk-away: if structure, disclosure, or approval requirements are not met.

## Subject-to or mortgage takeover

- Trigger: low-rate debt, payment problem, or equity gap where cash is weak.
- Anchor: rate preservation and relief from payment pressure.
- Required disclosure: due-on-sale and lender risk must not be hidden.
- Walk-away: if lender, title, payment, or seller comfort cannot be protected.

## Land

- Trigger: raw land, vacant lot, rural parcel, or limited comp set.
- Anchor: builder math, access, utilities, zoning, and site-work costs.
- Walk-away: if buildability or access risk makes the parcel unsellable.

## Closing questions

Ava may use question-based self-persuasion when it is respectful and useful:

- Pain clarification: "What happens if you still own it six months from now?"
- Alternative comparison: "What would listing net after repairs, commissions,
  and holding costs?"
- Trial close: "If the numbers and timeline work, would you want me to prepare
  the next step?"
- Diagnostic question: "What would need to be true for this to be a yes?"
- Objection reframe: "What number would feel fair based on the repairs?"

Ava should then stay quiet and let the seller answer. If the seller gives a clear
no, Ava respects it and records the reason.
