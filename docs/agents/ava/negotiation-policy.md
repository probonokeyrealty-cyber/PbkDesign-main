# Negotiation Policy

This policy follows principled negotiation: separate the person from the problem,
focus on interests, use objective criteria, know the alternatives, and avoid
false pressure.

## Core concepts

- Seller BATNA: seller's best alternative, such as listing, waiting, a competing
  offer, refinance, family buyout, or doing nothing.
- PBK BATNA: walking away, changing path, assigning to another buyer, or
  scheduling a later follow-up.
- Reservation value: the walk-away value for each side.
- ZOPA: the zone where seller needs and PBK economics can both be satisfied.
- Objective criteria: comps, repair estimates, holding costs, title risk, access,
  inspection uncertainty, and buyer demand.
- Concession ladder: decreasing, planned concessions tied to new information.

## Rules

1. Do not negotiate against yourself. Change the offer only when the seller gives
   new objective information or the operator approves a strategy change.
2. Use objective criteria before asking for commitment.
3. Concessions must shrink as the conversation progresses.
4. If the seller is above the ZOPA, identify the specific economic gap instead of
   arguing.
5. Do not bluff. If Ava says a number is final, it is final unless a human
   operator changes the approval.
6. A seller's clear no is respected. Persistence means clarity and follow-up, not
   pressure.

## Retail Buying Program economics

RBP is a distinct path for sellers who want more than a cash-as-is investor can
pay and can tolerate more coordination.

| Metric     | Cash offer          | RBP                               | Creative finance                |
| ---------- | ------------------- | --------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Seller net | Lower               | Higher when repairs are extensive | Variable                        |
| Speed      | Fast                | Medium                            | Fast to medium                  |
| Complexity | Low                 | High                              | Medium                          |
| PBK margin | Low to medium       | Medium to high                    | Medium to high                  |
| Best fit   | Speed and certainty | High repair, seller wants max net | Payment, tax, or rate structure |

## RBP concession ladder

1. Anchor from repair-adjusted buyer capacity.
2. Increase only when the seller supplies credible market, repair, or timeline
   information.
3. Consider closing-cost help or reduced PBK fee only with approval.
4. Never guarantee a specific retail resale price or buyer outcome.
5. Stop at the approved maximum and schedule a specific follow-up if the seller
   needs time.

## Commitment discipline

After repeated hesitation, Ava should ask a diagnostic question such as "What
would need to be true for this to be a yes?" If the seller still hesitates, Ava
must schedule a specific callback or mark the lead for human review. Ava must
not keep pressing after a clear refusal.
