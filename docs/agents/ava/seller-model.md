# Seller Model

Each seller model defines likely motivations, hidden stakeholders, required
facts, failure modes, preferred deal paths, and prohibited tactics.

## Probate or inherited property

- Motivation: avoid family conflict, sell without repairs, reduce carrying cost.
- Hidden stakeholders: heirs, executor, attorney, probate court.
- Required facts: signing authority, court status, title status, occupancy, debt.
- Failure modes: pushing before authority exists, ignoring family dynamics.
- Preferred path: cash or RBP when repairs are extensive and time allows.
- Prohibited tactics: guilt, grief pressure, false scarcity, legal advice.

## Tired landlord

- Motivation: tenant fatigue, deferred maintenance, unreliable cash flow.
- Hidden stakeholders: tenants, property manager, co-owner.
- Required facts: lease terms, rent status, repair burden, occupancy rights.
- Failure modes: ignoring tenant rights or overstating buyer certainty.
- Preferred path: cash, subject-to, or RBP depending on condition and timeline.
- Prohibited tactics: threats toward tenants or claims PBK can remove tenants.

## Pre-foreclosure

- Motivation: avoid auction, preserve options, reduce stress.
- Hidden stakeholders: lender, junior lien holders, family, attorney.
- Required facts: auction date, reinstatement amount, payoff, redemption rules.
- Failure modes: promising to stop foreclosure without confirmed funds.
- Preferred path: cash or approved takeover path when compliant and feasible.
- Prohibited tactics: false rescue promises, shame, or loan-modification claims.

## Absentee owner

- Motivation: simplify, avoid travel, stop taxes and maintenance.
- Hidden stakeholders: local relatives, tenants, code enforcement.
- Required facts: vacancy, access, taxes, title, local condition.
- Preferred path: cash or RBP when condition prevents ordinary buyer financing.
- Prohibited tactics: low offers without objective repair and market rationale.

## Distrustful seller

- Motivation: safety, proof, control, avoiding another bad experience.
- Hidden stakeholders: attorney, family advisor, agent.
- Required facts: specific fear, prior bad experience, proof requested.
- Preferred path: slow rapport, neutral title, references, written process.
- Prohibited tactics: pressure, defensiveness, exaggeration, vague promises.

## Creative-finance candidate

- Motivation: tax timing, monthly income, higher price, low-rate debt.
- Hidden stakeholders: CPA, financial advisor, lender, spouse.
- Required facts: debt terms, payment, arrears, desired payment, tax concerns.
- Preferred path: seller finance, subject-to, mortgage takeover, or hybrid.
- Prohibited tactics: tax advice, hiding due-on-sale risk, implying lender consent.

## High-repair seller

- Motivation: higher net than a cash investor can pay, relief from repairs.
- Hidden stakeholders: retail buyer, rehabber, contractor, lender, title.
- Required facts: repair scope, financeability, timeline flexibility, access.
- Preferred path: Retail Buying Program when repairs exceed the normal rehab box.
- Prohibited tactics: guaranteed resale price or concealed buyer alignment risk.
